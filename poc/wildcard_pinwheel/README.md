# Wildcard: rational-slope four-block pinwheel

Status: **measured, certified, and dominated.** This head does not beat the
incumbent at any tested `n`; it is worse than the incumbent *and* worse than the
trivial grid. The measured evidence and the reason are below.

## Construction

Four congruent `a x b` blocks of unit squares (`a >= b`) placed with 4-fold
rotational symmetry:

```
centre_k = R(k*90 deg) . (dx, dy),   angle_k = theta + k*90 deg,   theta = atan2(q, p)
```

with coprime integer slope `(p, q)` (the "rational slope"). Each block is rigid:
its `a*b` unit squares keep rotation `theta_k`, so the union is a genuine tilted
pinwheel, not a sheared or re-indexed grid. Leftover space inside the
axis-aligned bounding square is filled with upright unit squares on the integer
lattice.

Two centre families are scanned: `corner` (`(dx,dy) = (d,d)`, blocks toward the
four corners) and `side` (`(dx,dy) = (d,0)`, blocks toward the four sides).

## Capacity and side (exact bookkeeping, measured side)

- Block area: `4*a*b` unit squares (each block contributes exactly `a*b`,
  all rigid).
- Upright fill: every lattice cell whose separating-axis gap to all four blocks
  exceeds `1e-3` (float). Counted, not assumed.
- Capacity: `C = 4*a*b + #fill`.
- Side: measured translation-invariantly from the placed vertices,
  `L = max(max_x - min_x, max_y - min_y)`. It is **never** taken from area.

To emit exactly `n` squares, keep all `4*a*b` block squares and the
`n - 4*a*b` fill cells with the largest clearance to the blocks. `L` is
unchanged because the blocks alone determine the bounding box.

## Why this is not a repackaged failed approach

The failed list rules out thick tilted bands, the boundary zipper, hinged bands,
vacancy deletion, transplants, multi-D Pell sweeps, and `k^2+j` monolithic
strips. This is a *rigid four-body rotational* gadget: no band, no hinge, no
deletion, no Pell sweep, and no single monolithic strip. The whole shape is
generated by one slope and one centre distance.

## Bounded search

`31,405` configurations, all deterministic:

- `a in 2..12`, `b in 1..a-1`
- slopes `(1,0),(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(3,2),(1,2),(1,3),(2,3),(4,1),(1,4),(5,2),(2,5)`
- `d` on 30 points in `[0.3, 0.95(a+b)]`, both centre families

Capacity-matched configurations: **17,027** (`n=420`), **13,201** (`n=650`),
**8,336** (`n=930`). The minimum `L` for each capacity is in `data/search.json`.

## Verdict

- The best capcity-matched pinwheel is **worse than the trivial grid** at every
  tested `n` (`21.53 > 21`, `26.90 > 26`, `31.69 > 31`).
- The minimum-`L` member of the scanned family is always the **zero-tilt
  degeneration** `q = 0`. Every genuinely tilted member is strictly worse, so
  the slope itself is not buying anything here.
- Replacing the three incumbents with these packings would raise the total score
  by about `+0.0911` (distance from the target `22.19` grows). The construction
  is **dominated**, not capacity-mismatched: it can hit each `n` and still loses.

Reason: at `n <= 1000` the incumbents are only ~2.3–2.8% above the area lower
bound (area waste ~4.6–5.7%), while the 4-fold rigid pinwheel wastes boundary
area of order `L` (relative ~`c/L`, ~10% at these sizes). Tilting cannot shrink
the axis-aligned bounding square of a 4-fold symmetric set of rigid blocks; it
only rotates material into the corners, so the family is monotone-worse in the
tilt.

## Reproduce

```sh
python3 poc/wildcard_pinwheel/poc/run.py
```

Writes `data/pinwheel_{best,tilted}_n{420,650,930}.csv`, `data/results.json`,
and `data/search.json`. No repo-wide tests, sweeps, or edits to shared files were
run from this head.

## Limits

- The scan is bounded (the grid above), not exhaustive over all slopes or
  centre offsets; a non-4-fold or per-corner-different-angle arrangement
  (option b) is *not* covered by this negative result.
- "Dominated" is within the scanned 4-fold rigid family, and it is strong: the
  best member is below the trivial grid, not merely below the incumbent.
- No theoretical claim is made about optimal packings; only the measured
  certificates and the deterministic family scan are asserted.
