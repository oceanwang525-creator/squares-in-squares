Friedman's survey, https://kingbird.myphotos.cc/packing/squares.html, stresses that width-two and width-three diagonal strips must be off center; it also has non-45-degree and multi-angle examples. Gensane and Ryckelynck, Improved Dense Packings of Congruent Squares in a Square (2005), https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00454-004-1129-z.pdf, describe collision-free billiard moves, maximal feasible inflation, and collective center/angle perturbation followed by shrink and reoptimization. They explicitly warn about upright-grid jamming and difficulty finding several-angle optima. Neither reference gives a ready-made finite-n recipe that automatically beats Atlas.

My order of bets is deliberately qualitative, not invented probabilities. Best, though still uncertain, is a finite off-center thick-band generator with independent sub-band angles, asymmetric endpoint caps, and integer corner occupancy. Start from diverse exact-count seeds and explicitly solve the capacity deficit; sweep width, offset, phase and endpoints before free-angle relaxation. poc/hinged_band is a bounded CPU test of splitting the band into separately oriented pieces, not proof this beats Atlas.

Second is boundary-connected insertion with vacancy propagation. Open a seam that reaches the boundary, shift an entire contact component, insert extra squares, then shrink only when every wall extremum can move. This is not another internal-hole refill or coordinate-threshold cut. poc/boundary_zipper is a small lattice zipper construction test; its negative results must remain negative.

Third, lower odds in the available time, is a finite recursive trapezoid/interlock grammar with exact count accounting and finite-size error estimates, rather than invoking asymptotic density. First reproduce the full construction at small n and show that all boundary waste is included, then test n around 900. Existing finite first-stage failures make this less immediate than the first two. Do not rerun an unstructured annealer as a fourth idea.

Validation is part of the experiment. CSV angles are radians. L is the larger bounding range of actual square corners, not a solver's nominal container. A tolerance-based SAT test is not a certificate. Preserve exact decimal strings; require the unchanged official interval metric on both literal CSV and actual ordinary pandas read/write transport.
